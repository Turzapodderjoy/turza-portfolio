# Award / Gallery-Featured Personal Portfolio Websites

Research date: 25 October 2026 · Scope: individual personal/portfolio sites that are awarded or curated in the major design galleries.
Audience lens: recruiters, HR, founders, CEOs evaluating a professional personal brand (target persona: Turza Podder Joy — Denials Manager & Operations Analyst).

**Sources mined**
Awwwards (`/websites/portfolio`, `/websites/sites_of_the_day`, `/honors/portfolio`, `/websites/honorable`) · CSS Design Awards (`/`, `/wotd-award-winners`) · One Page Love (`/genre/personal` pages 1–4, `/genre/portfolio`) · Minimal Gallery (`/tag/personal`, `/tag/portfolio`, entry pages) · Land-book (`/design/portfolio`) · Httpster (`/`, `/type/design-and-illustration`) · Codrops (`/codrops/tag/showcase`, `/codrops/hub`) · Godly.website (now redirects to **recent.design**, `/websites`).

**Evidence key** — entries marked ◆ were fetched directly on the live site; the rest carry gallery metadata (genre tags, award date/score, listing position). Awwwards pages returned 403 to the fetcher, so Awwwards award data was collected via indexed award pages.

## Cross-governance notes

- **Cross-listing is common.** `noth.in` (Sara Guedj) appears on both CSSDA and Land-book; Belen Jones appears on One Page Love and recent.design; several Minimal Gallery "Personal" entries also sit in OPL. Being multi-listed is the norm for a top-decile personal site.
- **Godly.website now resolves to recent.design** — its `/websites` feed with a "Personal" and "Portfolio" filter is the direct successor for that gallery.
- **Codrops** (`tympanus.net/codrops/tag/showcase`, `/codrops/hub`) has effectively **no personal-portfolio gallery** any more — its showcase tag holds 3 long-form articles and the Creative Hub is tutorials/demos. Not a useful source for this brief.
- **Httpster has no "portfolio" type**; its closest categories are `Design & Illustration` and `Showcase`, from which individual-person sites were pulled.
- **Awwwards category structure to target:** `SOTD` → `Developer Award` → `Portfolio Honors` → `Honorable Mention`, in descending prestige. Monthly "Portfolio Honors" is the dedicated personal-portfolio track.

## Recurring design patterns observed

1. **Name-as-domain, title-as-headline.** The overwhelming convention is `firstname-lastname.com` (or a short nickname: `greymac.com`, `xander.xyz`, `btay.ca`) paired with a literal job-title line in the masthead — "Visual & Interactive Designer", "Product, UX & Growth Leader", "Creative Director", "AI Product Designer". Recruiters get role + name in under a second.
2. **Text-first, image-second heroes.** The award-winning tier (rauno.me, leoparpeix.com, Alexandre Araújo, Killian Herzer) opens with a single sentence of positioning and almost no chrome — no nav bar of six links, no stock hero photo. Restraint reads as seniority.
3. **Quantified proof blocks and live capacity signals.** Temitope Niyi shows `+50% client acquisition`, `18+ products built`, plus "Taking on 2 new projects this month". Samuel Räikkönen gives one measurable outcome per project (sales +24%, queues of 1h45m). Evidence beats adjectives for an operations/analyst audience.
4. **Narrative/structured CV as a first-class route, not a PDF afterthought.** Tim Van Damme ships a timeline + CV section; Eddy Naboulet exposes `02parcours.log` with a ⌘P print-to-CV; Atishay Tuli is filed under OPL's "Résumé" genre. A dedicated, printable, scannable career path is a recurring award-winning element.
5. **One signature gimmick, executed once.** Eddy-OS's CRT/terminal desktop, Half the Sky's plaster-relief carving, Maria Vasilyeva's playlist menu, Tim Van Damme's photocopied zine. The award-winning sites commit to a single metaphor rather than sprinkling effects — and the gimmick never blocks the actual content.
