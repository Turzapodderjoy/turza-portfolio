## Cross-cutting observations

### Recurring design patterns

1. **Fixed identity column + scrolling content.** Brittany Chiang's pinned name/title/one-liner on the left with experience scrolling on the right is the most-copied layout in the dev category; the same instinct shows up as a fixed nav, an availability banner, or a sticky one-line pitch elsewhere.
2. **Proof-first, above the fold.** Every strong roundup rewards sites that answer "what do you do / why you / how to hire" in the first screen: Irene Butenko's live-site thumbnail wall, Elise Dopson's client logo row + published rate, Andy Clarke's availability banner, Layor Salami's finding-style headings.
3. **Fewer projects, opinionated captions.** Colorlib and A1 Gallery both state it explicitly: 6–10 items, each captioned with client, role and outcome — not a uniform grid of 40. Descriptions carry a point of view ("rebuilt X around the idea that…") rather than metadata.
4. **Case studies as context → constraint → outcome.** The premium tier (Tobias van Schneider, Femke van Schoonhoven, Katie McCurdy, Jessica Goldman) writes the business result or the post-launch outcome first; screenshots are supporting evidence, not the deliverable.
5. **Availability / contact treated as a feature.** Banners, per-audience contact routing (Jean Jullien), rates on forms (Elise Dopson), pricing pages (Jessica Hische, Cheshire Property Studio) — the sites recruiters convert on make the next step explicit.
6. **Restraint reads as seniority.** Paco Coursey, Manu Moreale, Edmundo and Bráulio Amado prove that text-first, near-zero-chrome sites outperform decorated ones for experienced hires — motion is reserved for people whose craft *is* motion.
7. **The site itself as work sample.** Bruno Simon (3D game), Robby Leonardi (platformer résumé), Lynn Fisher (annual rebuild), UI Labs (lab framing): the medium demonstrates the skill instead of describing it.

### Relevance note for a Denials Manager / Operations Analyst portfolio

- The **most transferable** references are the non-visual-craft ones: Brittany Chiang (pinned bio + dated experience list), Tim Gesemann and Diogo Correia (timeline + side projects + stack tags), Elise Dopson / Kayla Voigt / Jen A. Miller (proof organized by audience/market), Jessica Goldman and Katie McCurdy (quantified outcome in the case-study title).
- Recruiters in healthcare RCM skim for: role clarity, measurable results (denial rate, DNFB, days in A/R, appeal overturn %), tooling (Power BI, Retool, Python), and a contact path — which maps exactly to patterns 2, 4 and 5 above.
- Avoid pattern 3's opposite (the fifty-thumbnail uniform grid) and any heavy WebGL/scroll theatrics; those signal creative-developer roles, not operations leadership.
